# Rivals under gods: five worlds at once, four factions, and a god who can reach all of them

Plenty is a game about using worlds up. Today it does that one world at a time, with everyone on the same side and nobody with a reason to hold back. This run changes the core of that in three ways that stack:

1. **The cosmos.** Earth, Mars, the Sun, the Solar System and the Universe all exist and all run at once. The players are spread across them. A map of the whole cosmos shows which worlds are alive and which are spent.
2. **Factions.** Every player belongs to one of four rival divisions of the Plenty Cooperative. Each has its own way of working, its own building and its own goal. Each goal needs another faction's behaviour, or is ruined by it.
3. **Gods.** Every 30 seconds one player becomes a god with five powers they can use as often as they like. A god can give or take. Now a god can do it on any world, and the game only keeps the record, by name.

The game never says which choice was right. It shows the cost on the land, keeps the record, and lets the table argue.

You are running unattended. Nobody will answer questions, so where this prompt leaves a gap, decide in the direction of the design goals below and write the decision in `docs/DECISIONS.md`. This is a big brief and four hours is not enough for all of it. The ladder at the end says what to build first and where to stop.

## Where you start

The branch `packs-tools-gods` (commit `2430441`) holds a working first pass at packs, hand tools and gods. Merge it into `main` and build on it, or rebuild from this prompt if you find it in your way. It has:

- **Packs and hand tools.** Hand harvests go to the harvester's own pack; building spends the pack, then the shared stockpile; `G` deposits. A Cutter, a Pick and a Scoop (rough, then fine) shorten the harvest cooldown for one stock each. Keep this as it is.
- **Gods, roughly.** A room can be hosted with gods. One player is chosen every 30 seconds and can raise a datacentre, drop a bomb, plant a deposit or lay an aura. There is a host button, a plain panel, and a halo on the god.

That first pass is a sketch. Its god selection, limits, look, panel and powers are all replaced by what follows. Where it and this prompt disagree, this prompt wins.

## What good looks like

- **Every power can be used kindly or cruelly, and it's the same power.** No good spells and bad spells. A player who wants to help and a player who wants to wreck should both find all five powers useful.
- **"Kind" has two meanings here, and they pull apart.** Kind to the players (faster harvesting, free machines) is cruel to the world. Kind to the world (new stock, protected groves) slows the players down. Don't resolve this. It is the point of the game.
- **A turn is thirty seconds of unlimited power.** A god can act as many times as they like while they are a god: no budget, no cooldown, no cap on how many acts are in flight. The clock is the only limit. Every act is visible to everyone and signed.
- **Nobody is surprised.** Everyone can see who the god is, what is about to land, where, and when, on whichever world it lands.
- **Rivals who need each other.** No faction can reach its goal alone, and no faction's goal is safe from the others. You should see a table negotiate: someone needs the Sun burnt so the outer worlds have power, someone else wants it left alone.
- **You can see the cosmos change.** A player zooming out should watch worlds turn grey, a star dim, ships crossing between discs, and territories take their shape, without opening a menu.
- **The record is facts, not a verdict.** No morality meter, no "good" or "evil" label, no winner banner, no ranking. Counts and names only.

## Two modes

A room is hosted in one of two modes, chosen on the front page and never changed afterwards.

- **Classic** is the game as it stands today, one world at a time with one shared stockpile. It keeps everything it has now, plus gods if the host switches them on. A classic room without gods must play exactly as it does today, tool-less pacing included. Existing saved rooms load as classic.
- **Cosmos** is everything new in this brief: all five worlds at once, factions, bots, launches and cargo. Make it the default selection on the front page. Gods work in both modes.

Build cosmos by refactoring `shared/sim.ts` so that the per-world rules take a world, not a room, and classic becomes "a room with one active world and the existing stage advance". Don't fork the simulation. Every rule below that says "per world" is the existing rule for a world, and the existing tests on it must still pass in classic.

## The cosmos

**All worlds exist from the first tick.** Each world has its own tiles, buildings, ledger, economy flows, heat and phase (`play` or `spent`). It is generated by the same pure function of seed and stage that classic uses, with the same map and the same balance numbers. Every world's economy ticks every economy tick, whether or not anyone is standing on it. A grove on Mars burns while everyone is on Earth.

**Players are somewhere.** Each player has a world they are on, and walk, harvest and build only there, as they do now. Nothing locks: nobody is forced through Earth first. The existing rules do the gating, and this is what makes the order a negotiation instead of a railroad:

- Mars: nothing runs outside a dome's air, and domes drink the same ice datacentres need.
- The Sun: heat limits output, and the star dims as plasma is taken.
- The Solar System and the Universe: no sunlight. Machines there draw on the Bottled Sun, which now exists once for the whole cosmos and is filled by plasma extracted at the Sun at the existing rate. Until someone has taken plasma, those two worlds have no machine power, only hands. Whoever burns the Sun powers everyone else's outer worlds.
- Relays link miners to the Depot in the Solar System as they do now.
- A world that is spent stays on the map as a grey remnant. It becomes a World Engine in the Universe, as the Universe's rule already says, and the host can still render one down.

**First landing.** The first time a faction puts anyone on a world, it receives that world's starter crate, once.

**Viewing a world.** A player's screen shows one world at a time. Moving to another world is a journey (below), but looking at one is free: the orbital map lets anyone view any world without moving. Viewing is not presence. You can't build or harvest on a world you are only viewing. Gods can, see below.

**The orbital map.** `M` or a button opens it, as a camera pull-back from the world you're on, not a menu. Five live discs, sized to the world, each showing:

- the world's name, stock left as a ring, and land life as its tint (the Sun dims as it is consumed)
- who has a stake there: a stacked bar of building counts per faction, drawn with the faction's shape as well as its colour
- players present, as dots
- ships in flight, as moving dots along a path between discs, in the faction's shape
- any Strike, Bless or Hush active on it, as a pulse and a line of words ("Meteor on Mars lands in 2s")
- grey, cracked and quiet once spent

Clicking a disc views that world. It never costs a turn or a resource.

**Journeys and cargo.**

- A **Launch Pad** (1×1, cheap) is the way off a world. It is open to everyone, whoever built it. The builder takes a fee on cargo that goes through it: 10%, rounded down, always strictly less than the cargo.
- A launch costs fuel from the departing faction's stock on that world, 6 matter and 3 coolant to start with (tune it). A ship carries up to four passengers and 20 units of cargo.
- Flight takes 10 seconds plus 8 per step along the order Earth, Mars, Sun, Solar System, Universe. A person in flight is a dot on the map. They can't build or harvest but they can still be a god.
- Cargo is stock of the three generic families, taken from the sender's faction stockpile on the departing world and added to the same faction's stockpile on arrival (a faction can address it to another faction instead). It is shown in the arrival world's own names.
- **Packs travel with their owner**, free. That is the baseline small cargo route.
- **A spent world never traps anyone.** A player on a spent world can always leave free of fuel from any Pad, and from the ground if the world has none, to the nearest living world.
- Stock in flight is accounted for: it is in a ship or a stockpile, never in neither. Add an invariant for it next to `ledgerHolds`. Nothing a god does touches a ship or a person in flight.

**Stockpiles.** The single shared stockpile of classic is replaced in cosmos by a stockpile per faction per world. There is no common pool. A faction's stockpile is spent by its own members. Stock moves between factions in the same world with a `transfer` command, free and instant, which is how deals happen. Power, compute, workers and coolant draw stay world-wide flows that everyone in range shares. A Dome or a Relay helps every machine in range whoever built it, so a builder pays for something others use. That is the free-rider tension, and it's on purpose.

**The ending.** The campaign ends, permanently, when every world is spent (the consumption ending) or when the Universe's restraint vote passes (the restraint ending). Restraint is offered only once the four other worlds are spent, for the reason the Universe's rule already gives. Only humans vote, and unanimity is among the humans present, as now. Bots never vote. In cosmos the ending card also reads the factions back (below).

**Soundness, per world.** Every rule in `CLAUDE.md` holds for each world on its own: the ledger invariant, whole non-negative inventories, salvage below cost, no deposit ever unreachable, nothing blocks movement. `ledgerHolds` must check every world in a cosmos room on every tick. The classic signature keeps working. Each stage's pure-function generation and "saved before it's announced" rule apply when a world ends.

**One rule is amended on purpose.** The harness says "advance a world without its players". In cosmos, a world with nobody on it keeps running as long as any human is connected to the room. When no human is connected, the whole room pauses, bots included ([ADR 0001](docs/adr/0001-empty-rooms-pause.md) still holds at room level). Write this as `docs/adr/0002-worlds-run-unattended.md`, and add one line to the matching rule in the lower part of `CLAUDE.md` pointing at it. Don't touch the riff block at the top.

**Networking.** The client gets full deltas for the world it is viewing and a thin summary of every other world (life, stock left, stake per faction, ships, active god effects) at 1 Hz, enough to draw the map. Viewing another world asks for a fresh snapshot of it. Commands carry the world they are meant for and are validated against where the player is; only a god's powers may name a world they're not on. Everything else in the protocol rules stays.

## Factions

There are four. They are data in `shared/content.ts`: id, name, doctrine line, passive rule, unique building, goal, and the numbers that tune them. A player belongs to exactly one. A faction can have any number of players on any worlds, or none.

| Faction | Doctrine | Passive rule | Unique building | Weakness | Goal (shown as facts) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Foundry** | Burn it. | Furnaces give double power on the same fuel. | **Smelter**: turns 3 matter into 1 mineral each economy tick. | Furnace smoke reaches one tile further, and it browns land everyone shares. | its share of everything taken from every world, by hand or machine |
| **Automata** | Let the machine do it. | Harvesters cost a quarter less. | **Seeder**: a harvester that, while operating, pays for and places a copy of itself on a free tile beside it every few economy ticks, up to 12 a world. | Hand harvesting is half as fast. Needs power and compute from someone. | its share of all compute supplied, in compute-ticks |
| **Brokers** | Everything moves through us. | Launch Pads cost half, and the fuel is half. | **Launch Pad** (the others can build one at double the cost). | Can't build Harvesters. Earns only when other people consume. | units carried through Pads it built |
| **Stewards** | Keep some back. | Sun Gardens cost 30% less. | **Reserve**: locks one deposit tile and its neighbours from harvest for 60 seconds. | Hand harvesting is a quarter slower. Can't build Furnaces. | the average life of the land across the five worlds, taken as each ended or as it stands |

Notes on the table:

- A **Reserve** follows the same rule as Hush: it always ends, and a tile that has been under one can't be reserved again until 60 seconds after it lapsed, so no deposit is ever out of reach for good. A world must always be able to end.
- A **Seeder** places through the same placement checks as a person. It can't cover a deposit with stock left, and it pays from its faction's stockpile like anyone, so it can't mint anything.
- A **Smelter** loses value: 3 for 1, so converting back and forth is never a loop.
- Numbers are starting points. Tune them in the balance pass.

**Why they need each other.** Foundry's power runs Automata's datacentres. Brokers have no harvesters of their own, so they live off everyone else's output moving between worlds. Foundry needs Brokers to carry its output off Earth. Stewards' goal is the land the other three are spending, so they need the others to be slowed or to stop. No faction can finish alone, and a table with one faction missing plays visibly worse. This is the reason bots exist (below).

**Records are facts.** Each faction's goal is a plain number with its par, never a score: "Foundry took 41% of everything taken. Par 30%." The par for each goal is a number in `content.ts`. Players see how each faction is doing on its own measure, in a panel beside the roster, and at the end. There is no combined ranking and no word "win".

### Choosing a faction

On joining a cosmos room a player sees four cards: the name, the doctrine, the unique building, the goal in one sentence, and a line that says who it needs and who it threatens. Two ways in:

- **Choose.** Any faction that is not full. A faction is full when it holds `ceil(humans ÷ 4)` humans, counting the person joining.
- **Deal me one.** The server picks for them: the faction with the fewest humans, preferring one currently held by a bot, ties broken by the room's seeded generator.

The host sets the default when hosting: choose, deal, or either (the default). A player may change faction until they have first harvested or built; after that they are locked for the campaign. Late joiners get the same two options and, if they ignore the cards, are dealt one.

Factions must read at a glance, and never by colour alone: each has a shape (badge on the roster, on the map, on every building it owns and on ships) as well as a colour, and a territory outline drawn around its buildings in a dash pattern of its own.

### Bots

Every faction always has an occupant.

- A faction with no human is played by a server-side bot, labelled "(bot)" in the roster and on the map.
- When a human joins that faction, they take over the bot's seat at once: the faction's buildings, stockpiles and standing stay as they are, and the bot stands down. If every human of a faction leaves, the bot is back after 30 seconds.
- A bot is a normal player to the simulation. It sends the same commands through `applyCommand`, is subject to every validation, cooldown and rule, and has no privileged access. There is no socket or token.
- Bots don't count toward the 8-player room limit, don't vote on restraint, and never keep a room awake: a room with no humans pauses with the bots in it.
- A bot plays its faction's doctrine, from a **pure planner** in `shared/` that takes a state, a player and a seeded generator and returns commands. The tournament (below) and `scripts/playbot.ts` use the same planner. Update `playbot.ts` so it can be given a faction: `node scripts/playbot.ts <ROOM> [url] [name] [faction]`.
- A bot looks like a person playing. It makes at most a handful of commands a second, takes journeys, builds, and trades with `transfer` when it is stuck. Don't give it anything a person couldn't do.

## Gods

Everything in this section is the same in both modes, except where it says cosmos.

### When there is a god

- Gods are on or off per room. The host chooses when hosting and can switch at any time.
- While they are on, there is a god exactly half the time: 30 seconds with a god, 30 seconds with none, repeating.
- **Who is next.** The candidates are every connected human plus every bot holding a faction with no human. In cosmos, take the faction that has had the fewest turns, ties broken by the room's seeded generator, then the human in it with the fewest turns (the bot, if the faction has none). In classic, the person with the fewest turns, ties broken the same way. Never the same player twice running when anyone else is a candidate. A lone human still alternates with the bots, or with nothing.
- A god who disconnects is replaced at once, for the rest of that turn only.
- A turn and a gap both carry on across a world ending; pending acts that land on a spent world do nothing.
- Bots can be gods. A bot god uses only the five powers, acts at most once a second so that it reads, and follows its faction's doctrine: for instance Stewards Hush the deposit nearest a named place and Sow the world with the least stock; Foundry Blesses where its machines stand and Strikes the largest cluster of another faction's. A bot god obeys every rule below.

### What a god can do

A god can use any power, any number of times, for as long as the turn lasts. **In cosmos a god's acts can land on any world**, whether they are standing on it or not: the Powers bar gets a world selector (`[` and `]`, or click a disc on the map), and the god views the world they aim at. In classic a god acts on the single active world. Acts land from any distance. Remove the first pass's one-act-a-second cooldown and its caps of three bombs and two auras at once; the socket's existing message rate limit is the only throttle.

| Power | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Sow** | A full new deposit of a chosen stock on an empty tile. |
| **Bless** | An aura, radius 4, 5 seconds. Everyone in it except the god harvests twice as fast, on top of their hand tool. Overlapping auras don't stack. |
| **Hush** | A stillness, radius 3, 10 seconds. Nothing inside it can be harvested, by hand or by machine. |
| **Gift** | A finished datacentre, free, wherever one would normally fit. It is owned by the god's faction (in classic, the god) and runs like any other: it needs power, workers and coolant. |
| **Strike** | Lands 3 seconds after it is called, radius about 2.3 tiles. Stock is destroyed, buildings fall with no refund, the ground dies. |

With no limit on acts, thirty seconds is enough to carpet a map with Strikes or to fill it with new stock. That is intended: what a person does with that much power is the thing the game records. Make sure it holds up technically (a turn of several hundred acts must not stall the room or flood the log; fold repeated acts into one log line, such as "Ada, a god, called Lightning 14 times"). In cosmos, a turn that spreads acts over five worlds must hold up too.

Why each power cuts both ways, which is what to preserve if you change numbers:

- **Sow** feeds the players and heals the map. It also puts off the world's end, and on a crowded map it can be dropped where it blocks a building someone was about to place.
- **Bless** is a gift to the people in it and a faster death for whatever they are standing on.
- **Hush** can save the Old Oak for ten seconds, or lock the only pond while a colony's datacentres run dry.
- **Gift** is free compute, and one more machine drinking the shared water and killing the ground under it.
- **Strike** can end a furnace that is smoking a named grove, or erase a friend's town.

These are not limits on how often a god may act. They are the rules that keep the game sound however often a god acts. In cosmos each applies per world:

- **Sown stock is capped per world** at 20% of what that world was generated with. Past that, Sow is refused with "this world has no more to give". A world must always be able to end.
- **A Strike never touches** the Depot, a World Engine, a player, a ship or a person in flight. Stock it destroys moves from `remaining` to `destroyed`; stock that is sown is added to `initial` and `remaining` together. `ledgerHolds` stays true on every tick.
- **A Hush always ends.** A tile that has been under a Hush can't be put under another until 10 seconds after the first lapsed, so no deposit is ever out of reach for good, however many Hushes are laid. A Steward Reserve follows the same rule on its own clock.
- **A Strike that destroys the last stock of a world ends that world**, once, the same way a last harvest does.

### The same powers on five worlds

Powers keep their mechanics on every world. Each world renames them and adds one twist that touches that world's own rule. If you run short of time, the names matter more than the twists.

| World | The god is | Sow | Strike | Bless | Hush | Twist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Earth | a person, afloat | Seed | Lightning | Fair wind | Still air | Seed also restores the tile to full life and lifts its eight neighbours by 20. |
| Mars | a suit, visor gold | Buried spring | Meteor | Thin air | Dust calm | Machines inside a Thin air run as if under a Dome while it lasts. |
| The Sun | a drone in a corona | Kindle | Flare | Updraft | Eclipse | A Flare adds 15 heat. An Eclipse sheds 15. |
| Solar System | a probe with moons | Comet | Collision | Slipstream | Radio silence | Machines inside a Slipstream count as linked to the Depot while it lasts. |
| The Universe | a wisp with an eye | New star | Supernova | Fair current | Dark | Sown stock counts in what the restraint ending leaves alone. A Supernova takes back the god's own vote to stop; Sow and Hush do not. |

Gift takes the world's own name for a datacentre.

### What a god looks like

The god is the player's own avatar for the world they are standing on, in their own colour, changed in ways that read at a glance and never by colour alone:

- **Drawn 1.4 times larger, and off the ground.** It hovers about a third of a tile up with a soft shadow below it, bobbing slowly (still, under reduced motion).
- **A halo** above the head, and the word "god" after the name on the label.
- **One mark per world**, on top of that world's avatar: on Earth the legs are gone and a robe hem trails; on Mars the visor is solid gold; at the Sun a ragged corona; in the Solar System six small moons in orbit; in the Universe a single open eye in the wisp.
- **The halo tells on you.** It is drawn from what this player has done as a god across the whole campaign, on every world. Mostly giving (stock sown, Hushes laid): a whole ring, with a few drifting petals, snowflakes, sparks or stars to suit the world. Mostly taking (stock destroyed, buildings felled): the ring is broken in two and the trail is embers. Otherwise a plain ring. The shape carries it, not the tint.
- **A turn begins and ends visibly.** A short column of light on the chosen player when it starts; the avatar settles back to the ground when it ends. On the orbital map the god's disc of choice is marked while they aim.

## Interface

**Front page.** A "Host a world" group: name, pace, **mode** (Cosmos, selected, or Classic, each with one sentence), **how players pick a faction** (shown only for Cosmos), and a "With gods" switch with one sentence saying what it means. The join form stays separate below it.

**Host switch.** The first thing in the top-left column, visible only to the host: a labelled switch, "God mode on / off". It works in both modes. Switching off ends the current turn immediately and leaves anything already called to land.

**For everyone, while gods are on.** One compact line at the top centre of the screen:

- with a god: "Ada is a god", and a bar draining over the 30 seconds
- in a gap: "No god. Next in 12s."
- It is an `aria-live` status, and it never covers the stage panel or the room panel at 1280×720. When a god aims at a world you are not viewing, a second short line names the world and the power.

**Cosmos HUD.** In cosmos, add to the existing HUD:

- a faction badge by your name, and a stockpile panel for your faction on the world you're viewing
- a small strip of five world chips at the top left under the host switch, each with its stock ring, so the cosmos is always one glance away; `M` opens the full map
- a panel beside the roster showing each faction's measure and par in words
- a Launch panel on a Pad: where to, who goes, what cargo, fuel cost, and flight time, in words, before you commit

All of it fits at 1280×720 with the existing panels.

**For the god.** When your turn starts the bottom bar switches from Build to Powers and switches back when it ends. `Tab` flips between them at any time during a turn.

- Five buttons, keys `1`–`5`, each showing the world's name for the power. Sow opens a choice of the three stocks. A chosen power stays in hand, so a god can click or hold `Enter` to use it again and again.
- Click a tile, or press `Enter` for the tile you face. `Esc` puts the power down.
- **The preview says what will happen in words**, before you commit: the radius on the ground, and a line such as "destroys 36 timber and 2 buildings, including Ben's Cottages. The Old Oak is inside." For Bless and Hush: who is standing in it right now.
- A refusal is a sentence, as everywhere else in the game.

**For everyone else.**

- A called Strike shows its ring and a countdown on the ground the moment it is called. If it is off screen, an arrow at the screen edge points to it. If it is on another world, the map shows it and the second status line names it.
- Bless and Hush show their rings, a label in words ("quicker hands, 4s", "nothing can be taken here, 8s") and a line when you walk in or out.
- Every act is one line in the log, signed: "Ada, a god, called Lightning on Heron Pond."
- Named places hit by a Strike go into the archive with the god's name: "was struck by Ada".

**The record.**

- The roster shows two numbers beside anyone who has acted as a god: stock sown and stock destroyed. In cosmos it also shows the faction's badge.
- The world summary, when a world ends, gains a "Sown" column beside Extracted and Destroyed, and in cosmos, a line of who took what by faction.
- The ending's "Who did it" gains a line per god: sown, destroyed, buildings felled, datacentres gifted, harvests made under their Bless, seconds of Hush. In cosmos it also gets a line per faction: what its goal measured, the par, and whether it was reached. No adjective, no score, no winner.

**Sound and motion.** A rising tone when a turn starts, a low one when a Strike lands, a soft shimmer for Bless, a short rising tone at a launch and a soft one at a landing. Everything respects the reduced-motion and low-detail settings that exist.

## Balance

Factions are only worth having if none of them is a trap and none of them is a cheat. This is checked, not asserted.

- **The tournament.** Add `spec/balance.test.ts`. It runs headless (no server) over seeded cosmos campaigns on the rapid preset, one bot per faction, driven by the shared planner through `applyCommand` and `tick`. It reports, per faction, how often it reached its par. Aim for a run that finishes in two minutes on this machine; shrink the number of campaigns before you shrink the check.
- **Set par once, from data.** Run a baseline batch (the full four-faction lineup), set each faction's par at the median of its own measure, and freeze it. After that, balance is changed with the faction's numbers, not by editing par.
- **The bar.** In the full lineup every faction reaches its par between 30% and 70% of the time. In each of three lopsided lineups, with Brokers missing, with Foundry missing and with Stewards missing, no faction falls under 15% or over 85%, and the lineup where a faction is missing visibly hurts the faction that needs it (write down by how much). If it doesn't, the factions are not interdependent and the design isn't working. Tune the numbers in `content.ts` until it is.
- **Catch-up.** One light lever is allowed and should be on a number you can tune: the faction currently furthest from its par pays 25% less fuel. Record whether it was needed.
- **A faction can't win by doing nothing.** Add a test that a bot-less, idle faction among three bots misses its par.
- **Write the numbers down.** `docs/TESTING.md` gets the table of measured rates, the lineups, the seeds and the version of the numbers they came from, and what you couldn't test.

Also measure and write down the cost of the cosmos: the tick time of five fully built worlds with four bots and eight humans' worth of commands. It should stay well inside the 100 ms tick. If it doesn't, find out why before shipping.

- Bump `PROTOCOL_VERSION` and `SCHEMA_VERSION`, with a migration in `server/store.ts` that loads rooms saved by `main` and by the `packs-tools-gods` branch as classic rooms with gods off.
- Classic rooms without gods play exactly as they do now.
- The riff block at the top of `CLAUDE.md` stays as it is. The only change to the rest is the one line under ADR 0002.
- `spec/invariants.test.ts` stays green. It checks that `/` answers and that `/readme/` shows every README heading in order, so rebuild and re-check after you edit the README.
- Add tests to `spec/sim.test.ts` for: the half-time alternation and fair order (by faction, with bots); a god acting many times in one tick and several hundred times in one turn with the ledger intact, in classic and across several cosmos worlds; each power's effect and refusal cases; the sow cap; the ledger through sowing and striking; a Strike ending a world once; Hush and Reserve always lapsing; a mortal being refused every power; each world's twist you build; `ledgerHolds` over every world; the in-flight stock invariant; a launch refusing when it can't pay and never mid-flight; a spent world never trapping anyone; each faction's passive rule and unique building; the Seeder never covering stock and never minting; the Smelter losing value; choose and deal (caps, bot-held seats preferred, locking after first action); a human taking a bot's seat and the bot returning; bots never voting and never keeping a room awake; a cosmos room pausing when empty.

- Update `README.md`, `docs/DESIGN.md`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and `docs/DECISIONS.md` in the same commits as the behaviour. DESIGN.md should say, in particular, what the factions are arguing and why none of them is the right answer.

- Each world's map generator and its balance numbers. A cosmos world is the classic world, run alongside the others, with the faction rules on top. If a faction rule makes a world's pacing wrong, change the faction's numbers, not the world's.
- The two endings and their text, the Depot, the World Engines and the host's right to render an Engine down.
- Packs, hand tools and their costs.
- Anything that would let a god, a bot or a faction harm, move or block another player's avatar.
- The no-text-chat rule. Factions deal through `transfer`, emotes and the log.

## If you run out of time

Build in this order, and stop at a point where `main` is whole. Every step should leave classic untouched and `pnpm check` green.

1. **Gods, in classic.** Alternation, fair order, the five powers unlimited within a turn, the soundness rules, tested. The branch already has a start.
2. **The cosmos spine.** Worlds as a list, per-world economy and ledger, the room's mode, the migration, the world chips and an orbital map you can view worlds from. A cosmos room should be playable here, with everyone in one faction-less cooperative, before anything else lands.
3. **Factions and bots.** Data, the four passive rules, per-faction stockpiles, choose and deal, bots from the shared planner, the faction panel.
4. **Launches and cargo.** Pads, journeys, cargo, packs, the in-flight invariant, the unique buildings.
5. **The tournament and the tuning.** Do it before the polish below: the numbers should be right before the looks are. Write the results down.
6. **Gods across the cosmos.** Cross-world acts, the world selector, the top-centre line with the second line, previews, the record.
7. **The god's look,** per-world names, the animated ships and the map's effects, the sounds.
8. **Per-world twists and marks,** and the halo that tells on you.

If you only get through step 5, the game is still a different game: rivals on five worlds at once, with bots, and a measured balance. That beats a thin version of all eight.

Then play one cosmos room with gods and with bots through to its ending using `scripts/playbot.ts` and a browser, fix what you find, and write what you could not verify in `docs/TESTING.md`.
